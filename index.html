Testosterone & Height

Testosterone speeds growth up. It also helps end it.

The pathway in five steps

  1. Testosterone rises. The testes make much more of it once puberty starts.
  2. Some becomes estrogen. An enzyme called aromatase converts part of it into estradiol.
  3. Growth speeds up. Early in puberty, both hormones fuel the growth spurt.
  4. Plates start to fuse. Later, estrogen signals the growth plates to harden into bone.
  5. Growth stops. Once the plates close, adult height is set.

In 1994, doctors described a 28-year-old man whose body couldn't respond to estrogen. His testosterone was normal, yet his growth plates had never closed. He stood 6'8" and was still slowly growing.

What actually shapes adult height

Genetics, mostly. Scientists estimate that about 80% of the variation in height comes from inherited DNA. Good nutrition, enough sleep, regular activity, and overall health during childhood and the teen years help a young person reach that potential. If a boy shows no signs of puberty by around age 14, or a child's growth seems to have slowed, a pediatrician can evaluate it.
